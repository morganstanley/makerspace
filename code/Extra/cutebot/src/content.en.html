From a beating heart to a robot that follows you

Four 60-minute lessons for 12-year-olds who have never coded. You don’t need to be a coder to teach it: every step is scripted, and every solution is shown as blocks.

Lesson 1 · first contact, no experience needed · 60 min

Hello, micro:bit

Pupils meet a tiny computer, write their very first program, and finish by making a badge that shows how they feel, with the robot’s lights changing colour to match.

Intro

Meet the micro:bit and MakeCode

10 min · no computers for 3 min

Hook3 min

“Hands up if you think there’s a computer in a phone. In a car? In a washing machine? In a birthday card that plays music? Computers are hiding everywhere, and today you get your own. It’s smaller than a bank card.”

Hand out the micro:bits. Let pupils find: the 25 red lights (the LED screen), button A and button B, and the USB socket. Tell them nothing will break if they touch it.

Tour7 min

On the projector, show the four parts of MakeCode. Pupils copy you on their computers.

  1. Open makecode.microbit.org → New Project → type a name → Create.
  2. The drawers (middle column): Basic, Input, Music… Each drawer holds blocks of one colour.
  3. The workspace (big white area): where you build. Drag a block in, drag it back to the drawers to delete it.
  4. The simulator (left): a pretend micro:bit. It runs your code instantly, so you can test without the real one.
  5. Download (bottom left): sends your program to the real micro:bit over the USB cable. The first time, click ⋯ → Connect device and follow the steps (this works in Chrome and Edge; in other browsers, drag the downloaded file onto the MICROBIT drive).

Say clearly: you cannot break anything by trying. Undo (the curved arrow) fixes any mistake.

1.1

Heartbeat

14 min · first block, forever loop

Hook2 min

“Put two fingers on your neck. Feel that? Your heart beats about 80 times a minute, all day, without you thinking about it. Today we give the micro:bit a heart.”

What does a heart do, again and again? (It gets big, then small, then big…)

Demo5 min
  1. A new project already has on start and forever. on start runs once, when the micro:bit switches on.
  2. Open the Basic drawer and drag show icon into on start. It clicks into place.
  3. Click the little picture and choose the heart. Look: the simulator shows it straight away.
  4. Download it to the real micro:bit together. This is everyone’s first program!

Build7 min

Challenge

Make the heart beat: big heart, small heart, big heart, small heart… forever.

  • Use the forever block instead of on start: it repeats without stopping.
  • You need two show icon blocks.
Solution
{{block:l1-1-solution|Solution: forever, show a big heart then a small heart}}

Watch out for

  • The block is floating on its own, not clicked in. Floating blocks look faded and don’t run.
  • Both blocks show the same heart: then nothing seems to move.
  • The download “does nothing”: check the cable is plugged in and that the device was connected.

Fast finishers

  • Add a pause block (Basic) to make a slow, sleepy heartbeat.
  • Make a racing heart: what makes it faster?

Name badge

14 min · text, order of blocks

Hook2 min

“At conferences, people wear name badges so others know who they are. Ours will be the only badge in the school that scrolls!”

The screen is only 5 lights wide. How can it show a long name?

Demo5 min
  1. From Basic, drag show string into on start. “String” is what coders call text.
  2. Click the word inside and type Hi!. Watch it scroll across the simulator, one letter at a time.
  3. Point out: the program runs top to bottom. The order of the blocks is the order things happen.
{{block:l1-2-example|Example: on start, scroll the text Hi!}}
Build7 min

Challenge

Make a name badge that scrolls your name, then shows a smiley face, again and again.

  • Which block repeats forever? You used it in the heartbeat.

1.3

Mood badge

19 min · events (buttons), extension, headlights

Hook2 min

“Your badge says your name. But how are you feeling today? A mood badge lets you change your face with the press of a button, and the robot will glow in your mood’s colour.”

If you were a colour right now, what colour would you be? Why?

Demo7 min
  1. Start a new project. From Input, drag on button A pressed. This is an event: the blocks inside wait until the button is pressed.
  2. Put show icon with a happy face inside. Press A on the simulator.
{{block:l1-3-example|Example: when button A is pressed, show a happy face}}
  1. Now meet the robot: slide the micro:bit into the Cutebot Pro’s slot, as shown in the kit’s instructions.
  2. Add the robot blocks: Extensions → search cutebot pro → choose Cutebot Pro by ELECFREAKS. A new teal drawer appears.
  3. Show set LED headlights all RGB lights color to and the colour picker. Don’t build the whole thing: that’s their job.
Build10 min

Challenge

Build a mood badge. Button A: happy face and a happy colour on the robot’s lights. Button B: sad face and a sad colour.

  • You need two button events: one for A, one for B (change the letter in the dropdown).
  • Switch the robot on to see the headlights.
Solution
{{block:l1-recap|Solution: button A shows happy and green, button B shows sad and blue}}

Watch out for

  • No teal Cutebot Pro drawer: the extension wasn’t added, or they picked plain “Cutebot”.
  • Two “button A” events: MakeCode greys one out. Change one to B.
  • Headlights stay dark: the robot’s power switch is off.

Fast finishers

  • Add a third mood on A+B (surprised? sleepy?).
  • Give each headlight its own colour with left RGB / right RGB.
Wrap3 min

Colour check: everyone presses a button at the same time and the room lights up. Pupils can pick any mood, real or pretend.

Lesson 2 · motors and lights · 60 min

Wake up your robot

The robot has been asleep in its box since the factory. Today it takes its first steps, learns to dance, and learns to drive with its buttons.

Warm-up: remember the mood badge? (3 min)

Put the lesson 1 program on the screen. Ask: “What made the face change?” (a button press, an event). Today the same buttons will make the robot drive. Pupils start a new project, so they add the Cutebot Pro extension again (remind them of the steps from 1.3).

2.1

First steps

18 min · wheel speed, pause, stop

Hook2 min

“This robot has never moved in its whole life. It doesn’t have legs. It has two wheels, and it has a clock. If I tell the wheels ‘go’ and then count to one... how far will it go?”

Hands up: how far will it go in one second? Write three guesses on the board.

Demo6 min
  1. Drag in on button A pressed from Input (just like lesson 1).
  2. From Cutebot Pro, add set LED headlights and pick green.
  3. Add set left wheel speed 50 %, right wheel speed 50 %. Explain: 100 is full speed, 0 is stopped.
  4. Add pause 1000 ms from Basic. “1000 milliseconds is one second.”
  5. Add stop immediately and turn off all LED headlights.
  6. Run it on the floor. Measure the distance with a ruler and compare with the guesses.
{{block:l2-1-example|Example: press A, green lights, drive forward for 1 second, stop}}

Key point: without the stop block the robot never stops. The wheels keep doing the last thing you told them. Try removing it (on the floor!) to prove it.

Build10 min

Challenge

Make your robot take one step forward with green lights, then one step back with red lights, then smile.

  • Hint: to go backwards, use a minus speed: −50.
  • Does it come back to exactly the same place?

Tell them to remember the colours: green means forward, red means backward. They come back in the final project.

Solution
{{block:l2-1-solution|Solution: forward with green, backward with red, stop, smile}}

Watch out for

  • Very low speeds may not be enough to turn the wheels. Start at 30 % or more.
  • It doesn't come back to the same spot: the wheels slip a little. That’s normal and worth a chat.
  • Pupils put the minus sign on only one wheel and the robot spins. Save that for 2.2!

Fast finishers

  • Predict, then test: how far does it go in 2000 ms? At 100 %?
  • Make it stop exactly on the parking line.
2.2

Dance party

18 min · turning, repeat loop

Hook2 min

“Our robot just got invited to a dance-off. Problem: it only knows ‘forward’ and ‘backward’. Let’s teach it to spin.”

Make two fists on the table, they’re the wheels. Both roll forward: we go straight. What happens if one fist goes forward and the other goes backward?

Demo6 min
  1. Set the left wheel to 50 and the right wheel to −50. The robot spins on the spot.
  2. Show that each headlight can have its own colour: pick left RGB and right RGB in the dropdown.
  3. Ask: “Which way will it spin if I swap the numbers?” Run it to check.
{{block:l2-2-example|Example: spin on the spot for 1 second with two different headlight colours}}
Build10 min

Challenge

Choreograph a dance: spin one way, then the other way, three times in a row. Change the headlight colours on every move, and finish with a heart.

  • New block: repeat 4 times from Loops. Change it to 3.
  • Moves inside the repeat happen again and again. Moves after it happen once.
Solution
{{block:l2-2-solution|Solution: repeat 3 times spin right in magenta then left in yellow, then stop and show a heart}}

Watch out for

  • The stop block ends up inside the repeat, so the dance stutters. Ask “what should happen only once?”
  • Pauses of 100 ms are too fast to see. Use 300 ms or more.

Fast finishers

  • With a micro:bit V2, add play melody from Music before the repeat.
  • Add a little hop: a short forward and back between spins.
  • Dance-off: two robots side by side, same code, who stays in sync?
2.3

Button driver

18 min · events, arrows

Hook2 min

“Until now the robot did a dance we planned in advance. Now you are the driver. In lesson 1, button A meant ‘happy’. What should button A mean for a car?”

If we press A and the robot starts driving, when will it stop?

Demo6 min
  1. Button A: north arrow, green lights, both wheels 40 %. No pause, no stop.
  2. Button A+B: stop, lights off, clear the screen.
  3. Press A and walk away. The robot keeps going: “the motors remember the last order.” The buttons ride on the robot, so the driver walks alongside to press A+B.
{{block:l2-3-example|Example: button A drives forward with a green arrow, A+B stops}}
Build10 min

Challenge

Finish your button driver: button B drives backwards with red lights and a south arrow. A+B stops and shows a square.

  • Parking game: start at the start line, park as close as you can to the parking line. Closest pair wins.
Solution
{{block:l2-3-solution|Solution: A forward green, B backward red, A+B stop with a square}}

Watch out for

  • Pressing A+B one after the other runs A or B first. Press both at exactly the same time.
  • The robot is plugged into the computer and driving off the desk. Unplug before testing!

Fast finishers

  • micro:bit V2: use on logo pressed (Input) to spin on the spot.
  • Use on shake to flash the headlights like a horn.

Lesson 3 · sensors and decisions · 60 min

The robot can see

Those two round “eyes” on the front aren’t eyes at all. They work like a bat’s ears. Today the robot measures distance and starts making its own decisions.

Warm-up: drive with the buttons from lesson 2 (3 min)

Pairs download their lesson 2 button driver and drive their robot for a few minutes. Ask: “Your robot drove into the wall last time. Why didn’t it stop by itself?” It can’t see yet, and that’s today’s lesson.

3.1

Bat ears

17 min · sonar sensor, forever

Hook2 min

“Bats fly in total darkness without crashing. They shout and listen for the echo. Our robot does exactly the same. One circle is a tiny speaker that shouts a sound too high for us to hear. The other is a microphone that listens for the echo.”

If the echo comes back really fast, is the wall close or far?

Demo6 min
  1. Introduce forever: “it runs again and again, as long as the robot is on.” A sensor needs to be checked all the time, not just once.
  2. Put show number inside, and drop the round sonar sensor unit cm block into its hole. Round blocks are values: they fit into holes.
  3. Move your hand in front of the robot and watch the number change.
{{block:l3-1-example|Example: forever show the sonar distance in centimetres}}

Distance detective (3 min game): call out a distance (“17 cm!”). Pairs race to hold a hand at exactly that distance, then check with a ruler.

Build9 min

Challenge

Turn the screen into a distance meter: the further away your hand is, the more LEDs light up.

  • New block: plot bar graph of … up to … (in Led). Put the sonar block in the first hole and 50 in the second.
Solution
{{block:l3-1-solution|Solution: forever plot a bar graph of the sonar distance up to 50}}

Watch out for

  • Soft things (jumpers, cushions) swallow the sound and give strange numbers. Flat, hard surfaces work best.
  • The sensor shows 0 when it hears no echo at all (nothing in front, or too far). Remember this for lesson 4!

Fast finishers

  • Change “up to 50” to 20. What changes?
  • What is the closest distance the sensor can measure? The furthest?
3.2

Parking sensor

19 min · if / else, else if, comparing numbers

Hook2 min

“When a car reverses into a parking space, it beeps faster and faster as it gets close to the wall. Real cars use the very same kind of sensor that’s on your robot.”

How does the car decide when to beep? What question is it asking itself, over and over?

Steer them to: “Is the wall closer than …?” That question is an if.

Demo7 min
  1. From Logic, take if … then … else. Put it in a forever.
  2. From Logic, take the 0 < 0 comparison. Put the sonar block on the left and type 10 on the right.
  3. If closer than 10: surprised face, red lights. Else: happy face, green lights.
  4. Read the program out loud as a sentence. Pupils love hearing that code can be read like normal words.
{{block:l3-2-example|Example: if the distance is under 10 cm show surprised and red, else happy and green}}
Build10 min

Challenge

Make a three-zone parking sensor: red ✗ when very close (under 10 cm), yellow when getting close (under 25 cm), and green ✓ when the road is clear.

  • Click the + at the bottom of the if block to add an else if.
Solution
{{block:l3-2-solution|Solution: under 10 red cross, else if under 25 yellow, else green tick}}

Watch out for

  • Order matters. If pupils check “under 25” first, the red zone never happens, because 5 is also under 25! Great 2-minute class discussion.
  • The sonar block is used twice. That’s fine today. In lesson 4 we fix it with a variable.

Fast finishers

  • micro:bit V2: add play tone (Music) in the red zone to beep like a real car.
  • Add a fourth zone with a new colour.
3.3

Mars rover

18 min · sensing + driving, obstacle avoiding

Hook2 min

“A rover on Mars has no driver. A radio message from Earth takes between 4 and 24 minutes to arrive, which is way too slow to shout ‘STOP!’. So the rover has to look and decide by itself.”

What two things should the rover do over and over again? (Look. Decide.)

Demo6 min
  1. Reuse the if/else from 3.2. This time the decision controls the wheels, not the screen.
  2. Closer than 15 cm: stop. Otherwise: drive at 30 %.
  3. Hold a book in front of the robot, then pull it away slowly. The robot follows the book and stops. (This is a sneak peek of lesson 4, so don’t tell them!)
{{block:l3-3-example|Example: forever, if closer than 15 cm stop, else drive forward at 30%}}
Build10 min

Challenge

Build a Mars rover that explores a maze of books. When something is close: red lights, back up, turn, and carry on exploring with green lights.

  • Build a little maze on the floor with books and bags.
Solution
{{block:l3-3-solution|Solution: if closer than 15 cm red, reverse half a second, turn; else green and drive forward}}

Watch out for

  • Don’t use show icon in a driving loop. Each picture takes about half a second, and the robot is blind and still driving meanwhile. Use the headlights to show what it’s doing.
  • The robot spins forever: the turn pause is too long or the speed too high.

Fast finishers

  • Use pick random (Math) to sometimes turn left, sometimes right.
  • Race: who escapes the maze fastest without touching a wall?
Wrap3 min

“Your rover runs away from things. Next lesson, it will do the opposite: it will follow you like a puppy, and stop before it bumps into you.”

Lesson 4 · variables and the final project · 60 min

Follow me!

First a shy robot that backs away, then a curious puppy that comes closer. Put them together and you get a robot that follows you and stops at just the right distance.

Warm-up: the human robot (5 min, no computers)

In pairs. One pupil is the robot and holds their hands out flat in front of them: that’s the sonar. The other holds up a palm as the target and walks around slowly. Rules on the board:

  • Far away → robot steps forward (say “green!”)
  • Too close → robot steps back (say “red!”)
  • Just right → robot freezes

Then: “Those three rules are the whole final program. Let’s teach them to the Cutebot, one rule at a time.”

4.1

Shy robot

13 min · variables, backing away

Hook2 min

“Our robot is shy. If you come too close, it backs away. But first it needs a memory. Right now it measures and forgets straight away. In the parking sensor we had to measure twice!”

When you measure something and want to remember it, what do you do? (Write it down. A variable is the robot’s notepad.)

Demo4 min
  1. Variables → Make a Variable… → name it distance.
  2. At the top of forever: set distance to sonar. Then show number distance.
  3. Picture it as a box with a label: every time round the loop, we throw out the old number and put the new one in.
{{block:l4-1-example|Example: forever store the sonar reading in a variable called distance and show it}}
Build7 min

Challenge

Make the robot shy: when you are closer than 15 cm, it backs away with red lights. Otherwise it stays still with its lights off.

  • Use your distance variable in the if, not the sonar block.
Solution
{{block:l4-1-solution|Solution: if distance under 15 back away with red lights, else stop and lights off}}

Watch out for

  • set distance placed in on start only measures once. It must be inside forever, at the top.
  • The robot backs away with nothing in front: that’s the 0 reading from 3.1 (no echo counts as 0, and 0 is under 15). Keep this for the final project.

Fast finishers

  • Make it extra shy: 30 cm.
  • Shy and polite: show a surprised face when it backs off (but remember the screen is slow!).
4.2

Curious puppy

13 min · “and”, ranges

Hook2 min

“Now the opposite: a curious puppy. When you walk away, it trots after you. But a good puppy doesn’t chase the wall on the other side of the room.”

Demo4 min
  1. Flip the shy robot: if distance is more than 25, go forward with green lights.
  2. Run it in an open space, pointing at a far wall. Oops: it drives all the way to the wall.
{{block:l4-2-example|Example: if distance is over 25 cm drive forward with green lights, else stop}}

How do we tell the robot “far, but not too far”?

Build7 min

Challenge

Teach the puppy to only follow you when you are more than 25 cm away and less than 80 cm away.

  • New block: and from Logic. Each side holds one comparison.
Solution
{{block:l4-2-solution|Solution: if distance over 25 and under 80 drive forward green, else stop}}

Watch out for

  • Pupils use or instead of and: then everything counts and the puppy chases walls again.
  • < and > mixed up. Read it aloud: “distance is greater than 25”.

Fast finishers

  • Hold a notebook and walk backwards slowly. Can you lead the puppy across the room?
  • Start the final project early!
Final

Follow me, Cutebot

24 min · everything together

Hook2 min

“You built a shy robot and a curious puppy. A shy puppy that is also curious... is a robot that follows you and keeps a safe distance. Just like the human robot game. Let’s build it.”

Plan it4 min

Draw this on the board. Every distance belongs to exactly one zone:

Why is there a “stay” zone? Ask what happens if “follow” and “back up” used the same number, say 20 cm. The robot would shake back and forth forever (try it as a fast-finisher experiment!). The gap gives it somewhere to rest. Heating thermostats use the same trick.

Build18 min

Final project

Program your robot to follow you. Too far: drive forward with green lights. Too close: back up with red lights. Just right, or lost: stop with blue lights.

  • Test with a notebook held low, at the height of the robot’s sonar, and walk slowly.

Give hints one at a time, only when a pair is stuck:

  1. Start from your Curious puppy program. It already does the green part.
  2. Click + on the if block to add an else if for “too close”.
  3. Copy the red blocks from your Shy robot into the else if.
  4. The robot backs away from nothing? Remember the 0: “too close” should be more than 0 and less than 15.
Solution
{{block:l4-final-solution|Final project: follow with green between 25 and 80 cm, back up with red between 0 and 15 cm, otherwise stop with blue}}

Watch out for

  • The sonar is low and narrow: legs and loose clothes give jumpy readings. A notebook works best.
  • The robot reacts late: check there is no show icon or pause inside the forever.
  • High speeds (above about 60 %) overshoot and bump into the target.

Fast finishers

  • The shaking experiment: set both numbers to 20 and watch.
  • Tune it: what speed and distances feel most like a real pet?
  • Sleep mode: button A switches following on and off (solution below).
Bonus: sleep mode with button A

A true/false variable following remembers whether the robot is awake. not flips it on every press.

{{block:l4-final-bonus-button|Bonus, part 1: button A switches following on and off, with a heart or a sleepy face}} {{block:l4-final-bonus-loop|Bonus, part 2: the follow-me loop only runs while following is true}}
Parade5 min

Everyone puts their robot on the start line, holds a notebook in front of it, and walks slowly across the room in a line. Robots that make it to the other side join the “Follow Me hall of fame” on the board.

“Four lessons ago, you made a heart beat. Today your robot can see you, decide what to do and follow you. Some robots in warehouses and hospitals do exactly this, with the same idea you just built.”

Troubleshooting

The problems you will see most, and what to check first.

What you seeLikely causeFix
No Cutebot Pro drawerExtension not added to this project, or plain “Cutebot” added.Extensions → search cutebot pro → ELECFREAKS Cutebot Pro.
Robot doesn’t movePower switch off, flat battery, or a speed too low to turn the wheels.Switch on, check battery, use 30 % or more.

Robot never stopsNo stop block: motors keep the last speed.Add set all wheel to stop immediately.
